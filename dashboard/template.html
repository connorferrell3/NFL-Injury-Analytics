Thesis

Has injury luck decided your team's season?

Every NFL team loses starters to injury, but not equally. We measure what each team's key players miss, in games and in cap dollars, and test whether the gap between the luckiest and unluckiest teams is random bad luck or a real, repeatable disadvantage. Then we turn that into something you can use each week: who is most likely to go down next, and how exposed your lineup is.

Six completed regular seasons, 2020–2025. Unless noted, "key players" are snap-share starters (defined below).

How the team comparison works

Each team compared on its own players, so a team with more stars isn't penalized for having more. "Cap dollars lost" is the share of those players' salary spent on games they missed to injury, so losing a $40M quarterback counts more than losing a $4M guard. The "Team top 30% by cap" definition gives every team the same share of players, which makes this the fairest comparison.

"53-man roster" counts everyone on a team that season who took a snap or was listed with an injury, so players who were cut or traded mid-season are included and the count runs above 53. Practice-squad players who never played are not.

How this was built

Snap-share starters

An offensive or defensive player who averaged at least 65% of his unit's snaps in the games he played (50% for running backs), minimum two games. Last season's starters also count if hurt early. About 22 per team.

Pedigree players

Any of: a first-round pick still on his rookie contract (up to year 5, ending early with an extension or new deal); a cap hit in the top 30% of his position group that season, from OverTheCap; a Pro Bowl or All-Pro selection the season before; or a spot on the NFL Top 100, the player-voted list released before each season. Prior-season honors are used because players who get hurt are less likely to be selected that same year; the Top 100 is voted on the previous season, so it has the same timing. Honorees are matched to rosters by name and team, so players who share a name (Josh Allen, Lamar Jackson) aren't mixed up.

Absences

A run of consecutive team games a player missed, counted only when there is injury evidence: an injury-related listing on the official report, or an Injured Reserve designation. Benchings, suspensions and personal absences are excluded. Releases and trades end the run. Absences count from the week a player joins the roster, so camp injuries (on IR or PUP, or simply listed Out) count from Week 1 even if he never plays. A team-by-team audit against raw rosters and injury reports matches 99.98% of injured player-weeks, 2020–2026.

Body region

Free-text report labels are mapped to 16 regions. Players sent straight to IR often never appear on the report while out, so the label comes from the week before or the practice report after they return. About 5% of absences, and a third of season-ending ones, still have no body part.

Repeat injuries

Next-season risk compares starters who were key players in back-to-back seasons. Same-region recurrence looks at each player's consecutive absences with a known body part; the chance rate is what you'd get if the next injury were drawn at random from the overall injury mix.

Fantasy

ADP from Fantasy Football Calculator's PPR 12-team drafts in the week before each season (2020–2025) and Sleeper's PPR ADP for the current season, since Fantasy Football Calculator only serves in-season drafts once the season starts. Weeks are counted from Week 1 even if the player never suited up.

Caveats

Current-season numbers cover only the weeks played so far and are kept out of the six-season averages. 2020 had a 16-game season and a separate COVID list, which is not counted as injury. Week 18 has extra one-game absences from playoff teams resting starters. Durations stop at the end of the regular season.

Data: nflverse injury reports, snap counts, weekly rosters, draft picks and OverTheCap contracts; Fantasy Football Calculator and Sleeper PPR ADP; Wikipedia Pro Bowl, All-Pro and NFL Top 100 lists. 2020 through the current regular season.
